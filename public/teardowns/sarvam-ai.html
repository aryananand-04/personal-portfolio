02 — North Star Metric

The metric that
actually matters

At ₹29.1 Cr revenue and $1.5B valuation, only one metric justifies the gap.

North Star Metric
Monthly API calls in production by paying enterprise customers
Sarvam is not a consumer product — it's infrastructure. The metric that matters is not Indus app downloads or GitHub stars. It's how many enterprise customers are calling Sarvam APIs in production, at scale, and paying for it. Every product decision should be evaluated against whether it increases production API usage by enterprises.

05 — Gap Analysis

Product gaps
RICE scored

Three gaps between Sarvam's current platform and what enterprise production customers need.

01
Critical
No self-serve enterprise tier — every customer requires manual sales
There is no self-serve path from API trial to paid production. With 114 employees, Sarvam cannot scale enterprise sales manually. A self-serve dashboard with usage-based billing, SLA tiers, and credit card checkout would allow Sarvam to convert developers without a sales team. This is the single highest-leverage product change — directly impacts the ₹29.1 Cr growth trajectory.

06 — Recommendations

Now / Next / Later
roadmap

Three initiatives to bridge the gap between ₹29.1 Cr revenue and $1.5B valuation.

Now · 0–3 months
Self-serve API dashboard with usage-based billing
Build a self-serve tier: sign up → get API key → usage dashboard → credit card billing → auto-upgrade to enterprise at threshold. Remove the "contact sales" gate for sub-₹1L/month usage. Fastest path to increasing the ₹29.1 Cr revenue line without hiring more salespeople.
KR1 — Self-serve revenue: ₹5 Cr ARR within 6 months of launch
KR2 — Developer-to-paying conversion: measure baseline, target 8%
KR3 — Time-to-first-paid-API-call: <30 minutes for 80% of sign-ups
Next · 3–9 months
Enterprise SLA page + BFSI vertical go-to-market
Publish formal SLA documentation and target BFSI as the first enterprise vertical. Banks need vernacular customer service at scale — Sarvam's STT + TTS in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu maps directly to this. The UIDAI partnership is the reference customer. Close 5 named BFSI logos in 6 months.
KR1 — BFSI enterprise contracts signed: 5 named logos in 6 months
KR2 — Enterprise ARR: ₹20 Cr from BFSI vertical alone
KR3 — Published SLA: 99.9% uptime commitment live within 4 weeks
Later · 9–18 months
Indus — reposition as India's voice-first government AI
Reposition Indus away from general assistant toward India-specific voice use cases: Aadhaar service assistance, government scheme eligibility, local language tax and banking help. Voice-first, regional language, government-integrated. Launch with 3 state government partnerships.
KR1 — Government partnerships: 3 state governments in 12 months
KR2 — Monthly active voice users on Indus: 5M in 18 months
KR3 — Indus revenue model: B2G licensing generating ₹10 Cr ARR
07 — PM View

The bottom line

"Sarvam appreciated 14x in under 30 months on ₹29.1 Cr of revenue. That's a bet on what it could become — not what it is. The product team's job is to close that gap before the next funding round needs defending."

The $1.5B valuation is a strategic bet, not a revenue multiple. Nvidia, Amazon, and Bessemer are betting on India's sovereign AI stack. That means Sarvam has 12–18 months to demonstrate a credible path to ₹200–300 Cr ARR before the narrative becomes a liability.
Open-sourcing Sarvam-30B and 105B was the right product decision. But open source requires a commercial layer on top. The Sarvam Startup Program is the beginning of that funnel. Self-serve billing is the next required step.
The UIDAI partnership is Sarvam's strongest asset and least-marketed one. Voice AI for Aadhaar services means every Indian citizen is a potential user. The product strategy should start from that use case, not from competing with ChatGPT in general reasoning.
